A. Prerequisites and staging

A1. Peritoneal cytology. Should the ascitic finding be treated as peritoneal metastasis? - Why now: the surgeon’s conditional statement of 13.10.2026 (“if confirmed, surgery is not indicated”) OPINION has not been commented on after the CSD review did not confirm it (14.10.2026). - Facts: Shalimov 06.10.2026, 3 smears, Romanowsky–Giemsa: clusters with marked nuclear polymorphism, category V FACT [196]. CSD LAB 14.10.2026, 2 of those slides, Pappenheim: low-quality sample, dense groups of small relatively monomorphic cells, no pleomorphism, AUS, origin undeterminable without a cell block FACT [197]. Ascites volume and peritoneal inspection: not documented (protocol not received). 2022 anamnesis: “sarcomatosis of the abdominal cavity, greater omentum” FACT [5]; 2025: ametabolic cystic peritoneal node over the uterus FACT [67]; 06/2026: small fluid in the pouch of Douglas FACT [70]; imatinib itself causes fluid retention with ascites in a minority of patients [26]. - Options: (i) repeat cytology on new fluid with cell block and CD117/DOG1/calretinin (needs a puncture or re-laparoscopy; a small-volume effusion may not be reachable); (ii) treat as indeterminate and proceed with planning (risk: operating on peritoneal disease); (iii) treat as positive (risk: withholding local therapy on a non-reproduced reading). Pooled risk of malignancy for AUS is 49 % (95 % CI 37–61 %) in carcinoma-dominated series, with poor inter-observer agreement for the atypical categories [2,27]; a cell-block immunopanel re-classified 86 % of AUS fluids in one series [28]; low-grade sarcomas rarely exfoliate into effusions [29]; in the published GIST effusion cases the tumour cells were large/pleomorphic and DOG1/CD117 positive on cell block [3,30]. - What changes the answer: the operation protocol (nodules seen or not); a cell-block result; interval imaging of the peritoneum. - Intra-operative practicality (parallel search, lit_peritoneal_staging/SOURCES.md; carcinoma data): laparoscopic inspection agrees with laparotomy on the presence of any peritoneal disease in about 96 % but under-estimates the extent; frozen section of peritoneal nodules is discordant in about 2.4 % (gastric cancer); rapid stains on washings take 21–54 minutes with sensitivity about 63–79 %; the definitive DOG1/CD117 cell block is a paraffin result. Washings should be taken into fixative for a cell block whatever the frozen-section result. - Who: cytopathology (both laboratories), the operating surgeon, medical oncology. - Literature: topic 4 — AUS pooled risk of malignancy 49 % [2].

I. Evidence summary (short; details in the topic sections)

  • Local treatment of limited progression on imatinib with the TKI continued is an option in ESMO and NCCN guidance [11,34]; outcomes after surgery depend on the response pattern at operation (responsive ≫ unifocal > multifocal progression) [14] and on completeness of resection [16]; a registry analysis associated local treatment with better OS in selected patients [36].
  • No published series was identified for drainage, aspiration or sclerosis of a cystic GIST liver metastasis; the related literature concerns rupture, haemorrhage and the definition of tumour rupture [35].
  • Ablation adjacent to the portal pedicle: thermal techniques carry heat-sink and biliary-injury risk; IRE has small series near major ducts [5]; histotripsy has no GIST data and a ≤ 3 cm pivotal-trial population [8].
  • Transarterial therapy and radiotherapy have small GIST series (topics 5–6); the only prospective GIST radiotherapy study stabilised 80 % of irradiated lesions [7].
  • Peritoneal cytology: the AUS category carries a pooled risk of malignancy near 50 % with poor reproducibility; cell block with DOG1/CD117 is how GIST cells are identified in fluid [2,3].
  • Systemic therapy: imatinib interruption after response leads to early progression [13]; regorafenib must be withheld ≥ 2 weeks before elective surgery [12]; the exon 13 + 18 co-mutation is a subgroup without a clear winner between sunitinib and ripretinib [1].
