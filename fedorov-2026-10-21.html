FOR DOCTORS. Summary of a family telephone call with the surgeon made on the day of the operation, 21.10.2026 (recording 13:44), when the operation was finishing. Organised by topic; not a transcript. This is a verbal report, not an operation protocol or a histology result.

OPINION Everything attributed to Dr Fedorov on this page was said in an informal telephone call, not written in a signed document. The operation protocol and the pathology report of the resection have not yet been received.

Key points (opinion, as told by the surgeon)

  • The planned right hemihepatectomy was performed, with a marginal (edge) resection of the stomach; the primary gastric focus was removed.
  • The right-lobe tumour, which contains fluid and is easy to injure, was removed without rupture.
  • Two additional foci resembling dissemination were found in the lesser omentum and on the anterior abdominal wall. One was sent for an intra-operative (express) biopsy: no obvious tumour cells were seen. Both were removed as a precaution. The surgeon said the final answer is possible only after the full histology and that he cannot yet say whether they are metastases. They were not seen at the staging laparoscopy.
  • Blood loss was small. A small volume of viable liver tissue was removed from the right lobe; the left lobe remains.
  • The left-lobe lesion is not small; the plan is to wait for liver regeneration in order to remove it (second stage). Until then the treatment is likely to continue, either the current drug or a second line, at the oncologists' discretion.

Details

The operation

  • OPINION Right-sided hemihepatectomy as previously discussed, plus a marginal gastric resection; the primary focus removed.
  • OPINION Rupture of the right-lobe tumour was a recognised risk (it contains fluid and its wall is easily injured); it did not occur.
  • OPINION Minimal blood loss; the operation went normally.
  • OPINION A small volume of viable parenchyma was removed with the right lobe; the left lobe remains.

Additional foci and pathology

  • OPINION Two additional foci resembling dissemination (lesser omentum; anterior abdominal wall) were seen; they were not visible at the primary staging laparoscopy.
  • OPINION One focus went to an express biopsy; no obvious tumour cells were seen there. The surgeon discussed this with the pathologist (named as Vlad K. in the call), who said a final decision is possible only after the full histology. The surgeon did not want to say now whether they are metastases. Both were removed to be safe.
  • The brother said he had written to the pathologist asking that the 05.10.2026 liver biopsy of the left lobe be compared with the resected right-lobe material when it arrives (family statement); he plans to speak with the pathologist when the material reaches the laboratory.

Left lobe, treatment and mutations

  • OPINION The left-lobe lesion is about as large as the right-lobe one ("not small"); removal depends on regeneration of the remaining liver.
  • OPINION In the meantime, therapy is likely to be continued or a second line started; this is for the oncologists to decide.
  • Family hope: removing the right-lobe disease might remove one mutation (clone) entirely so that a lighter drug could be used. OPINION The surgeon: "God willing", but he cannot say; it is not certain that the mutations are split between left and right.
  • OPINION Imatinib: resumption "as soon as it is allowed, she recovers and eats normally" (answer to the brother's question). No date was given.

Condition and immediate recovery

  • OPINION At the time of the call the patient was still under anaesthesia; the skin sutures were being placed, about 30 more minutes.
  • (family report) Asked whether the skin closure was the same as the heavy stitches she felt after the laparoscopy: OPINION the surgeon said the heaviness is her subjective sensation and the stitches are normal. He added something about thread (suture) closure being worse; the wording is unclear in the recording.
  • OPINION Nothing additional is needed at the moment. If any medications or supplies are needed, the family will be told. The surgeon noted that the surgical team has a heavy schedule and is not always available, so answers will be given but are not urgent.
  • (family report) The patient chose not to move to a single-bed room and wants to be with other people; a four-bed ward. OPINION The surgeon: a bed can be changed, new beds have just arrived; nothing radically better is possible.
  • Diet: (family report) the brother found the food-order menu (tables 5 and 4) and said the patient could not eat porridge with milk or sausages after the laparoscopy, which he attributes to imatinib (family interpretation). OPINION The surgeon: this will be decided as things go.
  • OPINION The edge of the stomach containing the tumour was cut off. The patient will probably not be able to eat solid food at first; the surgeon cannot say what will happen later but patients usually can eat normally after this, not immediately. The team will say what she can eat.
  • OPINION Asked whether the operation would be published, the surgeon said they do not record every operation. About the tumour: it is "a tumour like any other, it is just advanced".
