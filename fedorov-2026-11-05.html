FOR DOCTORS. Summary of a family telephone call with the surgeon on the day of discharge, 15 days after the first-stage operation. Organised by topic; not a transcript. The written discharge summary of the same day: translation.

OPINION Everything attributed to Dr Fedorov on this page was said in an informal telephone call, not written in a signed document.

Key points (opinion, as told by the surgeon)

  • Things are going well. Ascites was present for a while; the drain has been removed and fluid is not re-accumulating for now.
  • The plan is unchanged: control tests at home, then the oncologists decide on further drug treatment, then CT to assess the liver remnant (regeneration) and whether the left-lobe tumour has grown, then planning of further treatment.
  • The Shalimov oncologists are in favour of regorafenib. The surgeon noted that the drug is prone to complications, bleeding among them, and that this should be discussed with the oncologist. His advice: follow the treating oncologist who has known the patient for a long time, since the Centre's oncologist saw her once.
  • Ablation or embolisation of the left-lobe lesion is not possible; this was discussed before, and it would have been chosen if it were possible. He cannot say how active the left-lobe tumour is until he sees the CT.
  • CT not earlier than about the end of November: the liver needs that time to regenerate. If no drug is started, about 2–3 weeks from now.
  • Desmoid fibromatosis: rarely seen by him; his first thought at surgery was carcinomatosis from the GIST, but the pathologist states it is fibrous tissue only. If so, at the extent found (small foci) it is insignificant and poses no threat.

Details

  • OPINION In about one week: abdominal ultrasound, complete blood count and biochemistry (proteins, albumin, the same tests as in hospital), at a local laboratory; standard panels. Albumin is the main indicator to follow, more than the liver enzymes.
  • OPINION She can eat normally, as before the operation, and live at home. Avoid physical strain. Someone should stay with her, to notice if she feels unwell; no problems are seen now, this is a precaution.
  • OPINION If the oncologists consider the drug needed, she starts it; as he understands, a course is about 2–3 weeks.
  • (family report) The family is waiting for the genetic results on the resected material before the drug decision, and plans to stop regorafenib about two weeks before a second operation.
  • INTERPRETATION The discharge summary of the same day recommends regorafenib 160 mg once daily for 21 days and a contrast CT in 1 month (translation), consistent with the surgeon's timing.
