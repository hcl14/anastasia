FOR DOCTORS. Summary of a family telephone call with the surgeon one week after the first-stage operation of 21.10.2026 (right hemihepatectomy, cholecystectomy, wedge resection of the stomach[202]). Organised by topic; not a transcript.

OPINION Everything attributed to Dr Fedorov on this page was said in an informal telephone call, not written in a signed document. The operation protocol, the discharge summary and the written histology report have not yet been received.

Key points (opinion, as told by the surgeon)

  • Recovery is going generally well: slower than after a standard resection, but this was not a standard resection, so for her case it is normal. Blood tests are good; he sees no critical problem now.
  • Main current issue: ascites draining through the abdominal drains, which is common after such an extensive resection; the volume is falling, from almost 2 L at the start to about 1 L now. It still needs correction and may continue for some time; patients are sometimes discharged with the drains if everything else is fine.
  • Imatinib has already been restarted (by 28.10.2026).
  • Next steps: wait for the final histology, immunohistochemistry and genetic tests, after which the oncologists decide on systemic options; then imaging not earlier than about one month (3–4 weeks) after the operation. This is a first assessment, not automatically the second operation.
  • Preferred imaging for the surgeons: PET-CT with intravenous standard (iodinated) contrast, or a separate contrast CT, so that the anatomy of the remaining liver and the residual lesion can be assessed. A liver-specific MRI contrast is mainly for small seedlings, which PET already shows.
  • The left-lobe lesion touches the vessels of the left lateral section (inflow and outflow). If it does not grow, a limited enucleoresection keeping part of the left lateral section may be possible (small chance). If it grows, the likely option is a left lateral sectionectomy (segments II–III), which depends on how well segment IV regenerates. The left-sided operation will be the more aggressive one.

Details

Course after the operation

  • OPINION Slower recovery than after a standard resection is expected for this extent of surgery; for her case it is going normally. Blood tests good.
  • OPINION Ascites through the drains: almost 2 L at first, about 1 L now; decreasing, still needs correction; may go on for a while; discharge with drains is sometimes possible if all else is fine.
  • OPINION Ultrasound: small fluid collections, normal after this type of operation, no correction needed. The examination results will be given together with the discharge summary.
  • OPINION Skin sutures not yet removed; they will be removed; standard closure, nothing to worry about.
  • OPINION Discharge: when she can look after herself and her condition is compensated; the trend is positive. Asked about roughly one more week, the surgeon said he hopes for about that, but cannot predict.
  • OPINION Bowel function: re-establishing stool is a process that may take time, and it was a problem before the operation as well. (family report) The patient has been managing chronic constipation (which the family attributes to imatinib) with large-volume enemas (about 2 L). OPINION The surgeon called 2-L enemas a bad idea (they wash out the gut flora; "a prosthesis, not a function") and suggested more dietary fibre or stimulating micro-enemas.

Gallbladder

  • OPINION Cholecystectomy is a standard step of a right hemihepatectomy: the cystic artery arises from the right hepatic artery, so after removing the right hepatic artery the gallbladder cannot be kept functional. The alternative would be a smaller, non-anatomical resection. (Same explanation as in the chat of 26.10.2026.)

The resected right-lobe tumour

  • OPINION The tumour occupied practically the whole right lobe, with a small island of parenchyma on top that also contained tumour. It replaced the parenchyma; she did not lose a large amount of functional parenchyma with this resection.
  • OPINION Thick capsule with central necrosis and tumour along the capsule and outer layer — this was already visible on CT. Depth of infiltration cannot be judged macroscopically; that is for the pathologists.
  • OPINION Margins: the team kept as far from the tumour edge as possible, but in places the resection went almost along the edge of the tumour, because a wider margin would have compromised the liver remnant.
  • OPINION Vessels: unlike hepatocellular carcinoma, the tumour has no single feeding artery; vessels form by neoangiogenesis at the periphery as it grows from the centre outwards, so the centre loses supply and becomes necrotic.

Remaining left-lobe lesion and the second stage

  • OPINION Probably little or no necrosis inside yet; ultrasound shows a fluid component that is still microscopic — an early stage of the same process.
  • OPINION It contacts the vessels of the left lateral section (inflow and outflow). Options depend on its growth: unchanged → small chance of enucleoresection preserving part of the left lateral section; larger → left lateral sectionectomy (segments II and III), leaving segment IV, which depends on segment IV regeneration. If the remnant regenerates enough to allow the more radical removal, "that is very good".
  • OPINION Imaging in about a month assesses the residual tumour and the remaining parenchyma (whether it is sufficient for the second stage); the decision on what is done next and when is made after the histology/genetics, the oncologists' view and this imaging.
  • OPINION For systemic-therapy decisions PET may be better; for surgical planning (morphology: what liver can be left) the PET should include intravenous standard contrast. (family report) No allergy to iodinated contrast.
  • OPINION Whether tumour growth can be slowed: a question for the oncologist, once the final immunohistochemistry and all tests are available.

Pathology and genetics

  • (family report) The brother is in contact with the pathologist; after consulting the oncologist he asked for all available histology/IHC and the genetic tests on the resection (three genetic tests, as stated by the brother).
  • OPINION The surgeon: let the oncologists review the results and say whether the new tests open systemic-treatment options; then, with the CT, the next step and its timing will be decided.
