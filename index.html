I. Evidence summary (short; details in the topic sections)
  • Local treatment of limited progression on imatinib with the TKI continued is an option in ESMO and NCCN guidance [11,34]; outcomes after surgery depend on the response pattern at operation (responsive ≫ unifocal > multifocal progression) [14] and on completeness of resection [16]; a registry analysis associated local treatment with better OS in selected patients [36].
  • No published series was identified for drainage, aspiration or sclerosis of a cystic GIST liver metastasis; the related literature concerns rupture, haemorrhage and the definition of tumour rupture [35].
  • Ablation adjacent to the portal pedicle: thermal techniques carry heat-sink and biliary-injury risk; IRE has small series near major ducts [5]; histotripsy has no GIST data and a ≤ 3 cm pivotal-trial population [8].
  • Transarterial therapy and radiotherapy have small GIST series (topics 5–6); the only prospective GIST radiotherapy study stabilised 80 % of irradiated lesions [7].
  • Peritoneal cytology: the AUS category carries a pooled risk of malignancy near 50 % with poor reproducibility; cell block with DOG1/CD117 is how GIST cells are identified in fluid [2,3].
  • Systemic therapy: imatinib interruption after response leads to early progression [13]; regorafenib must be withheld ≥ 2 weeks before elective surgery [12]; the exon 13 + 18 co-mutation is a subgroup without a clear winner between sunitinib and ripretinib [1].

Guideline and evidence basis for treating the focal progression locally

  • NCCN Guidelines, Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors (von Mehren M et al., NCCN Guidelines Insights: GIST, version 2.2022, J Natl Compr Canc Netw 2022;20(11):1204–1214, jnccn.org; NCCN Guidelines for Patients: GIST 2023, PDF): for limited progression on imatinib the options are to continue the TKI and add resection (if feasible), ablation, embolization or chemoembolization of the progressing lesion(s), with palliative radiotherapy for symptomatic lesions (category 2B); a switch to the next-line TKI is reserved for generalised progression. Resection of metastatic disease "may be beneficial in patients on imatinib or sunitinib who have evidence of radiographic response or limited disease progression".
  • 2026-09-23 — PET-CT whole body (low-dose CT, no contrast) — English translation Imaging translation on Drive [hosted copy] (Ukrainian original [hosted copy])
    Kyiv City Clinical Oncology Centre (KMKOC) original (scanned PDF)
  • ESMO–EURACAN–GENTURIS clinical practice guideline (Casali PG et al., Ann Oncol 2022;33(1):20–33, doi:10.1016/j.annonc.2021.09.005): in case of limited progression on imatinib, treatment of the progressing lesion (surgery, ablation) with imatinib continued, or imatinib dose escalation, are the recommended options before changing the systemic drug.
  • Dutch GIST Registry, multicentre analysis (Brink P, Kalisvaart GM, Schrage YM et al. Local treatment in metastatic GIST patients. Eur J Surg Oncol 2023;49(9):106942, doi:10.1016/j.ejso.2023.05.017, PubMed): of 457 metastatic GIST patients, 123 had elective surgery or ablation; local treatment was associated with better overall survival (HR 0.56, 95 % CI 0.34–0.93). After local treatment, disease confined to the liver predicted better survival (HR 0.27) and progression during systemic treatment predicted worse survival (HR 3.9) — so the benefit is largest when the disease is liver-limited and responding, and the current focal progression on imatinib is exactly the setting in which patient selection matters most.
  • GIST Support International, "Radiofrequency ablation (RFA)" (gistsupport.org): RFA "is applicable to relatively small liver tumors that are not close to blood vessels or ducts in the liver" and can destroy a resistant clone while imatinib controls the rest; cited series: Dileo et al. 2004 (nine patients, limited progression sites, all lesions completely ablated, no complications), Hasegawa et al. 2007 (three patients with focal liver progression, progression-free at 8, 15 and 16 months), Pawlik et al. 2006 (RFA ± surgery vs surgery alone: higher recurrence with RFA, reflecting selection), Avritscher and Gupta 2009 (RFA "a viable palliative option for patients with advanced GIST who develop focal progression … during imatinib therapy"). The same source's proximity caveat is the reason thermal ablation was judged contraindicated here (contact with the left portal branch and bile duct), which leaves resection, SBRT, IRE or transarterial treatment as the guideline-compatible local options.

Full evidence summary (systemic options, local options, timing, immunotherapy)
  • Pattern: imatinib-refractory, liver-limited oligoprogression ("nodule within a mass") driven by polyclonal secondary KIT resistance; the exon-11 bulk disease still responds to imatinib. A combined local-plus-systemic strategy is favoured over simply switching TKI, and imatinib should continue throughout any local therapy (stopping the TKI collapses the benefit of ablation).
  • Systemic options: sunitinib covers V654A but not A829P; regorafenib and ripretinib cover A829P and, per VOYAGER ctDNA data, regorafenib remains clinically reasonable against V654A; sorafenib has the broadest in-vitro coverage but only retrospective GIST data. Prior PRES with intracranial haemorrhage raises the stakes for potent VEGFR inhibitors (sunitinib, regorafenib); if one is used, start at a reduced dose with tight blood-pressure monitoring. Trial referral is recommended because no approved single agent covers the full profile.
  • Liver-directed options for a viable nodule inside a cystic mass: arterially directed therapies (DEB-TACE/TACE, Y-90 TARE; NCCN 2A) treat only perfused tissue; microwave ablation (after cyst aspiration) or cryoablation for a few well-defined nodules; SBRT for a perivascular/central nodule that is unsafe for a probe; IRE is the vessel/duct-sparing alternative for a nodule abutting major vessels or central bile ducts (GIST data anecdotal); RFA is the least suited technique in fluid-containing targets.
  • Timing: ablation planning needs multiphase CT or MRI no older than about 30 days; the June scan is stale and the 32 % ctDNA fraction argues for re-imaging now and definitive local therapy within roughly a month, provided the disease is still oligoprogressive.
  • Not realistic now: mRNA neoantigen vaccines and TIL therapy (no GIST data, low TMB); checkpoint inhibitors alone give about 16 % response in TKI-refractory GIST.
  • Other: the 31 mm lucent right femoral-head lesion mentioned in the 08.07.2026 LISOD read should be characterised (MRI) because extrahepatic disease changes the value of arterial therapies; the 08.09.2026 expert reading found no bone lesions.
