Formulated diagnosis

ICD-10: C16.9 (malignant neoplasm of stomach, unspecified); ICD-O morphology 8936/3, gastrointestinal stromal tumour.

Diagnosis: Gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) of the stomach, KIT exon 11 deletion (Q556_V559>H), with metastases to the liver (bilobar, dominant right-lobe mass) and peritoneum, stage IV. Initially (08/2022) diagnosed and treated as leiomyosarcoma of the uterine body T2NxM1; diagnosis revised 04–06/2023 on histology (DOG-1, CD117 positive; Israeli pathology revision of all samples). Concomitant: uterine leiomyomas; right ovarian endometrioma; type 2 diabetes; iron-deficiency anaemia.

08/2022 — liver-metastasis biopsy read as metastatic uterine leiomyosarcoma; bilobar liver masses, paragastric mass 51 × 46 mm, pelvic masses.
09/2022 – 01/2023 — status after 4 cycles gemcitabine + docetaxel + bevacizumab: progression in the liver (MRI 10/2022, CT 12/2022). Oncomine (12/2022): KIT exon 11 deletion (VAF 42.5 %), subclonal KIF5B–MET fusion, TMB low, MSS; germline panel negative.
05–12/01/2023 — pazopanib 800 mg, stopped on day 8: posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome with left occipital haemorrhage, hospitalised 13–25/01/2023, resolved; CT 09/02/2023 with 12 % regression.
01/2023 — repeat liver biopsy: reported as metastasis of uterine leiomyosarcoma.
02–03/2023 — status after 3 cycles trabectedin: progression on PET-CT 11/04/2023.
04–06/2023 — EUS-FNA of the paragastric mass and liver core biopsy: GIST of the stomach, GIST metastasis in the liver (CSD, 21–26/04/2023); Israeli revision 04/06/2023 confirms GIST in all samples.
03/05/2023 — imatinib 400 mg/day started. 08/2023 — marked metabolic response (PET-CT). 03/2024 — stable. 05/2025 — stable, "PET negative", cystic residues.
06–09/2026 — PET-CT 19/06/2026: return of enhancing, FDG-active mural components in the known liver lesions after 3.5 years of imatinib (LISOD re-read 08/07/2026; Israeli expert reading 08/09/2026: metabolic progression, left-lobe lesion 42 × 36 mm, right-lobe mass ~15 × 10 cm, no bone lesions).
08/2026 — FoundationOne Liquid CDx (ctDNA fraction 32 %): KIT exon 11 Q556_V559>H (17.0 %) plus secondary KIT exon 13 V654A (8.8 %) and exon 18 A829P (4.0 %) — polyclonal imatinib resistance.
08/09/2026 — recommendation: sunitinib 50 mg 4/2 (regorafenib 160 mg d1–21 as an option); not yet started; imatinib continues. 10/09/2026 — thermal ablation of the left-lobe lesion judged contraindicated (contact with the left portal branch and bile duct); other local options under discussion.

Disease course at a glance

Patient. Anastasia Zherlitsyna, born 25.04.1976 (50), Kyiv.

Diagnosis. Metastatic gastric GIST with liver metastases, treated as uterine leiomyosarcoma T2NxM1 from 28.09.2022 until the April–May 2023 biopsies established GIST (DOG-1 and CD117 positive; Israeli revision 04.06.2023 confirmed GIST in all three 2023 samples).

Sites of disease (19.06.2026 PET-CT, expert reading 08.09.2026). Dominant right-lobe liver mass about 15 × 10 cm, cystic/necrotic residue with increased peripheral uptake; left-lobe segment II/III lesion 42 × 36 mm (own CT measurement 46 × 48 × 42 mm, 27 mL), metabolically silent in 2024–2025 and FDG-active in June 2026 — the current progression; calcified, near-inactive gastric-wall residue 37 × 31 mm; uterine leiomyoma, stable, low activity; no bone lesions.

Current treatment. Imatinib 400 mg/day since 03.05.2023 (started in hospital 3–8 May 2023), continuing. On 30.07.2026 and again on 08.09.2026 the LISOD oncologist recommended a switch to sunitinib 50 mg, 28 days on / 14 off, with regorafenib 160 mg days 1–21 q28 as an alternative — sunitinib has NOT been started as of 11.09.2026.

Organ and safety context. PRES on pazopanib in January 2023 with left occipital intracerebral haemorrhage and multifocal vasoconstriction, largely resolved by June 2023 — this is why potent VEGFR inhibitors carry extra risk. Blood pressure rose to 150–160 mm Hg before that episode. Baseline echocardiogram before sunitinib, 30.07.2026: normal, EF 67 %, no wall-motion abnormality. Comorbidities on record: type 2 diabetes, iron-deficiency anaemia.

Open clinical question. Local treatment of the FDG-active left-lobe lesion. On 10.09.2026 an interventional specialist judged thermal ablation (RFA/MWA) contraindicated because the lesion contacts the left portal branch (0.8 mm; 0 mm to the active component) and the accompanying left bile duct. TARE, TACE/DEB-TACE, SBRT, IRE and resection of segments II–III are under discussion, with imatinib continued.

  • Which local modality, given contact with the left portal branch and duct: SBRT, IRE, superselective DEB-TACE/TARE, or resection of segments II–III — with imatinib continued.
  • Systemic choice after local therapy: sunitinib as prescribed, regorafenib, ripretinib or a trial — weighed against the PRES history.
  • Whether to biopsy the active nodule for tissue NGS, to confirm the two resistance clones are in separate cells (trans).
  • Timing: ablation planning needs imaging no older than about 30 days, and the 32 % ctDNA fraction argues for acting within roughly a month.

Current situation (as of 11 September 2026)

Patient: Anastasia Zherlitsyna, born 25.04.1976 (50), Kyiv, Ukraine.

Diagnosis: metastatic gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) of the gastric wall with liver metastases, KIT exon 11 deletion (Q556_V559>H).

Molecular status (most important): the FoundationOne Liquid CDx blood test of 20.08.2026 (ctDNA tumour fraction 32 %) found two new KIT resistance mutations on top of the original exon 11 deletion: exon 13 V654A (VAF 8.8 %) and exon 18 A829P (VAF 4.0 %), with the exon 11 driver at VAF 17.0 %. This is polyclonal imatinib resistance; the report flags imatinib, sunitinib and avapritinib as resistant and lists regorafenib as NCCN category 1 — see the drug table for which drug covers which clone and all genomic findings from both tests. First treated (from 28.09.2022) as uterine leiomyosarcoma T2NxM1; the fourth biopsy (April–May 2023) established GIST. Uterine masses are considered leiomyoma; right ovarian endometrioma.

Where things stand (September 2026): after 3.5 years of response to imatinib, the June 2026 PET-CT shows renewed activity in the liver, concentrated in a left-lobe lesion that touches the left portal branch. A blood genomic test (FoundationOne Liquid CDx[75]) explains the resistance (two new KIT mutations on top of the original exon 11 deletion found by Oncomine[11] in 2022). Sunitinib was prescribed on 30.07.2026 and re-confirmed on 08.09.2026 (with regorafenib named as an option), but has not been started as of 11.09.2026; local treatment of the active lesion is being explored and thermal ablation has been judged unsafe.

Interventional-radiology opinion (thermal ablation specialist, 10.09.2026, via messenger)

Thermal ablation (RFA, MWA) is contraindicated for this lesion because two contraindications coincide: proximity to the left branch of the portal vein and, most importantly, direct adjacency to the left bile duct. Y-90 radioembolisation (TARE) costs from about EUR 40,000 and is not performed in Ukraine (nor in Czechia or Slovakia) for that reason. The family's questions about transarterial therapies (DEB-TACE, TACE, TARE) and about surgical resection remain open.

Assessment against the imaging: consistent. The lesion abuts the left portal branch and the metabolically active tumour is exactly on that side, so an ablation margin would have to include the branch and the accompanying duct.

Full evidence summary (systemic options, local options, timing, immunotherapy)
  • Pattern: imatinib-refractory, liver-limited oligoprogression ("nodule within a mass") driven by polyclonal secondary KIT resistance; the exon-11 bulk disease still responds to imatinib. A combined local-plus-systemic strategy is favoured over simply switching TKI, and imatinib should continue throughout any local therapy (stopping the TKI collapses the benefit of ablation).
  • Systemic options: sunitinib covers V654A but not A829P; regorafenib and ripretinib cover A829P and, per VOYAGER ctDNA data, regorafenib remains clinically reasonable against V654A; sorafenib has the broadest in-vitro coverage but only retrospective GIST data. Prior PRES with intracranial haemorrhage raises the stakes for potent VEGFR inhibitors (sunitinib, regorafenib); if one is used, start at a reduced dose with tight blood-pressure monitoring. Trial referral is recommended because no approved single agent covers the full profile.
  • Liver-directed options for a viable nodule inside a cystic mass: arterially directed therapies (DEB-TACE/TACE, Y-90 TARE; NCCN 2A) treat only perfused tissue; microwave ablation (after cyst aspiration) or cryoablation for a few well-defined nodules; SBRT for a perivascular/central nodule that is unsafe for a probe; IRE is the vessel/duct-sparing alternative for a nodule abutting major vessels or central bile ducts (GIST data anecdotal); RFA is the least suited technique in fluid-containing targets.
  • Timing: ablation planning needs multiphase CT or MRI no older than about 30 days; the June scan is stale and the 32 % ctDNA fraction argues for re-imaging now and definitive local therapy within roughly a month, provided the disease is still oligoprogressive.
  • Not realistic now: mRNA neoantigen vaccines and TIL therapy (no GIST data, low TMB); checkpoint inhibitors alone give about 16 % response in TKI-refractory GIST.
  • Other: the 31 mm lucent right femoral-head lesion mentioned in the 08.07.2026 LISOD read should be characterised (MRI) because extrahepatic disease changes the value of arterial therapies; the 08.09.2026 expert reading found no bone lesions.
