Surgical consultation — condensed summary

18 September 2026 · Dr Denys Fedorov, Ukrainian surgeon · resectability of the liver metastases in metastatic gastric GIST

TimelineDocuments

Bottom line

Two-stage liver resection is on the table, and it is the only route discussed that could be radical. Step 1: right hemihepatectomy (takes the 15 × 10 cm mass and the segment I lesion). Step 2, after the left lobe regenerates: removal of the segment II/III focus, or the whole segment III. Not in one stage — risk of liver failure. First action: new PET-CT; if stable, proceed.

Left-lobe focus

  • Sits between the left hepatic vein and the segment III branches, wrapping the vessels. Open question: how deeply it invades them.
  • Superficial invasion → resectable with a margin; all liver metastases could then be removed surgically.
  • Deep invasion found during stage 2 → more liver has to go than planned → liver-failure risk. This is why it is never done in one stage.
  • Options for stage 2: targeted removal leaving a cavity in the parenchyma (technically feasible, not simple), or resection of the whole segment III.
  • The decision is made after stage 1 plus a repeat CT, once the left lobe has regenerated — clearer then, though not 100 %.
  • What the radiologist called a left-lobe lesion is in fact segment I; it can be removed during the first resection.

Palliative resection (debulking)

  • Acceptable on its own: "we remove the tumour load — that is treatment too". Described as effective for GIST, though not radical.
  • The right-lobe mass comes out with the right lobe.
  • Caveat he raised himself: a two-stage operation with risks at both stages is justified mainly if the goal is radical resection. For debulking alone, he questions whether oncologists would endorse it — the case must pass a multidisciplinary tumour board, with an argument for the patient's benefit.

Stomach (primary tumour)

  • Metabolically controlled. Technically easy to remove.
  • Could be taken out during the same palliative operation, but he would rather not: tissue healing after extensive resections is worse, with anastomotic-leak risk.

Before any major resection

  • PET-CT first. If the picture is stable and there are no contraindications, continue with the plan.
  • Staging laparoscopy — his recommendation: rule out peritoneal nodules that "MRI and CT do not see". Non-traumatic, inspection of the abdominal cavity, biopsy if anything is found.
  • Order: basic workup first (ultrasound, echocardiography and so on), then laparoscopy as a staging step. Basic tests can be done quickly.
  • Bones: suspicious foci were described earlier — needs imaging and a specialist opinion on all extrahepatic foci before committing.

Sceptical about

  • Embolisation — works on hypervascular tumours; hers is hypovascular ("dark on CT") → limited.
  • Radioembolisation / radiointerventional treatment — not targeted enough given the volume: resorption of a large amount of tumour parenchyma, which can turn into an abscess.
  • Histotripsy and "apoptosis of tumour cells only" — "that does not work".
  • Electrochemotherapy (as offered in Ukraine) — electrodes too short, not for the liver.
