FOR DOCTORS. Summary of a family telephone call with the surgeon on the day of the control ultrasound at the Shalimov Centre, four weeks after the first-stage operation of 21.10.2026. Organised by topic; not a transcript. Blood tests of the day before: translation.

OPINION Everything attributed to Dr Fedorov on this page was said in an informal telephone call, not written in a signed document. No written ultrasound report is on file.

Key points (opinion, as told by the surgeon)

  • Ultrasound 18.11.2026: all well. A small strip of fluid, normal after such operations; no changes that would worry the team. Ultrasound cannot show liver regeneration or the state of the tumour and cannot replace CT.
  • Contrast CT of the abdomen as soon as possible — from next week. Enough time has passed; the regeneration that was to happen has happened. It can be done at the Shalimov Centre (to be booked in advance; there is a queue).
  • PET-CT is still needed (booked for 24.11.2026): CT assesses the state and volume of the liver parenchyma, PET looks for new foci outside the liver. Combining PET with IV contrast may be unusual for the PET centre; if it is not possible, a separate CT.
  • Blood tests (low albumin and total protein, raised eosinophils): expected after a large operation and with this disease, both affecting the liver; they do not stand in the way of surgery. Nothing catastrophic. There is no adequate blood marker for this tumour; imaging decides.
  • Second stage. The question is whether the remaining disease is resectable when the decision is made. If an operation is offered, it will be heavier and more complex than the first, with higher risks: it removes a large part of the liver, with a substantial probability of post-operative liver failure (jaundice, infections, coagulopathy), and her condition afterwards may be considerably worse than after the first operation.

Details

  • OPINION Liver transplantation is a different treatment and does not apply: the disease is disseminated. It could be discussed for a tumour primarily confined to the liver; it is described for metastases too, but the risks are entirely different.
  • OPINION Nutrition. Sip feeds (Nutridrink) are the right decision. They reduce appetite, so they should be taken after a normal meal, "like a medicine"; powdered and liquid formulas are equivalent balanced enteral nutrition. No tablet for appetite as such. (Target about 2500 kcal/day, surgeon, 12.11.2026.)
  • OPINION Herbal choleretics, asked about by the patient: not evidence-based; no objection, but their effect is unknown, and she no longer has a gallbladder, which such remedies usually target.
  • OPINION A repeat liquid biopsy after the operation is unlikely to change anything.
  • Context (patient report): imatinib not taken since 15.11.2026; appetite and weakness still a problem; regorafenib not started. The surgeon did not comment on systemic treatment.
