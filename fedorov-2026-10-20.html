Key points (opinion)

  • State clinic within the national programme of medical guarantees (NHSU); about 200 such operations a year against 1–2 in low-volume clinics; ultrasound, angiography and cardiology on site.

Hospital stay, sutures and recovery course

Sutures and length of stay

  • Family question: about the sutures.
  • Standard sutures are used; they will not hinder her in any way.
  • Dr Fedorov's impression (hedged, "it seems to me"): the patient expects the course to be like after the diagnostic staging laparoscopy.
  • After the right hemihepatectomy she will stay in the clinic for no less than one to one-and-a-half weeks.

  • Post-operatively her condition will be limited by the operation itself, not by the skin sutures, which are "not such a big factor".

Early post-operative period

  • Family question: what she will be like after the operation, and whether she will be unconscious. The question about consciousness was not answered directly.
  • For a certain time she will have restricted movement and will be lying down; as a rule this lasts several days.
  • She will be on ward regime. She will lie in bed not because the doctors want it, but because getting up will most likely be hard for her.
  • Family question: whether this is because of pain. Answer: yes, there will be post-operative pain, which can usually be controlled with analgesics.
  • Restricted physical activity after the operation is a natural process and cannot be otherwise; a person cannot get up and run after half of the liver has been removed.
  • If all goes well, patients after open operations then return to normal activity, with restrictions.
  • It will not be like after the staging laparoscopy, when she went for a 3 km walk the next day; she must understand that this is a major intervention with restrictions.

Operation risks

  • Any liver resection, especially with this pathology, carries a certain risk; not all post-operative risks can be predicted.
  • Deviation from the plan: the operation may depart from the planned order; intra-operative findings may change the course of the operation and the team's actions.
  • Usually this does not happen; there are few risks in principle, but they exist potentially.
  • Tumour rupture during the operation is the most unpleasant risk. The team hopes it will not happen, but it cannot be excluded 100 %.
  • Reason: the tumour looks like an abscess — thin tumour walls with necrosis inside.
  • Bleeding: such operations usually proceed without large blood loss, but this cannot be excluded either; it can occur intra-operatively.
  • The clinic has the necessary blood components available for that case.
  • Dr Fedorov corrected himself: not blood loss as such but bleeding is a fairly dangerous complication that can lead to undesirable consequences, and the family should understand this.
  • Wound infection: such patients have a risk of post-operative wound infection, related not to the clinic's sanitary state but to weakened immunity in cancer patients, who are more vulnerable to infectious complications.
  • Unpredictable course: how the post-operative period will go cannot be said before the operation; it usually goes normally, but there is always a risk of deviation from the normal course.
