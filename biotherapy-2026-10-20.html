FOR DOCTORS. Summary of a family telephone call about keeping living (cryopreserved) tumour tissue from the planned operation. Organised by topic; not a transcript.

Source: telephone call of 20.10.2026 (about 19:10; family and the consultant); informal statements of a commercial service, not a written document, not medical advice of this site. Prices are the company's quoted service prices, as stated in the call (estimates, not an invoice).

Site assessment (28.10.2026): overstated claims, likely misleading — not recommended

COMMENTARY The consultant's statements in this call were checked against the company's own website, its brochure, trial registries and PubMed. The key claims are not supported, and several are contradicted by the company's own materials:

  • "Not clinical trials, scientifically proven treatments." The company's own German brochure (2024) describes the methods as experimental procedures not yet approved by evidence-based medicine, offered as "compassionate therapy", and states that Israeli law restricts their clinical use, which is why patients are treated in Germany and Kazakhstan. The Kazakhstan page also cites "compassionate use".
  • "We engineer the viruses (from the medical reports)." The company's oncolytic-virus page lists two ready-made avian viruses, Newcastle disease virus and infectious bursal disease virus, with no per-patient modification described.
  • Evidence cited by the company: the only registered trial of its virus therapy, NCT00348842 (Hadassah), was withdrawn in 2006 before enrolling anyone; no results. The other publications cited are a cell-culture study (PMID 27724977), a mouse study (PMID 7489566), reviews by other groups, a conference abstract, and a single-author case series of donor-lymphocyte therapy in blood cancers (PMID 37202579). None reports outcomes of its vaccines or virus therapy in patients, and none concerns GIST.
  • "We have such [GIST] patients." No mention of GIST or imatinib anywhere in the company's patient stories, reviews, sarcoma, stomach-cancer or partner-clinic pages. No published report of oncolytic-virus treatment of GIST in humans was found.
  • Treatment sites: the German partner is a small private complementary-medicine practice in Rheinfelden (co-owned by the physician who carries out the protocols prescribed from Israel); the Kazakh partner's address is a general private multi-profile medical centre in Almaty. No clinical-trial registration, ethics approval or published results from either site were found.
  • Other statements in the call: the Tel Aviv bank was called "the only facility" that keeps tumour tissue alive (other commercial tumour banks exist); the consultant suggested, "off the record", carrying the frozen sample in checked luggage, although import of human tissue into Israel needs the Ministry of Health permit he himself described.

COMMENTARY Conclusion of this site: the offer (personalised vaccine, about $45,000–100,000; oncolytic-virus therapy, about €54,000) is marketed with overstated and partly false claims of proven efficacy for experimental, unapproved methods without published outcome data. It is not part of the treatment plan and is not recommended. For context, the only human data on this virus (a different Hadassah team, strain NDV-HUJ): a phase I/II trial in 11 patients with recurrent glioblastoma, one complete response (PMID 16257582).

Question asked. The family wanted to keep part of the tumour removed at the operation planned for the following day alive (liquid nitrogen), in addition to the usual formalin fixation, for possible future trials. A Ukrainian laboratory had said it could freeze tissue in nitrogen but could not store or transport it.

Key points

  • The company stores living tumour tissue only in its own tumour bank in Tel Aviv; the stated purpose is to prepare a personalised anti-cancer vaccine from fresh tissue (preparation takes 3–4 months).
  • Surgeon's part, as stated: the tissue goes at once into sterile tubes with a small amount of saline and is kept frozen on dry ice (−70 to −80 °C) or in liquid nitrogen (−196 °C); viability is checked on arrival at the bank.
  • Import into Israel needs a Ministry of Health permit, about 1–2 months. Transport from Ukraine would need a courier; the company has no logistics partner in Ukraine at present and collection from Ukraine was described as the hardest step. Interim storage at a partner (Germany/Turkey) was not offered; Germany to be checked by the consultant.
  • Paraffin blocks can be sent for their other options; the family stated that a biopsy in paraffin had already been shipped to Israel by courier without problems.
  • Outcome (family statement): the living-tissue option will not be pursued now because the logistics cannot be arranged in time; formalin fixation only.

Quoted prices (company statements, estimates)

  • Storage of living tumour tissue in the Tel Aviv bank: $3,600 for up to 3 years. A shorter 2-month storage price was not given (to be checked by the consultant).
  • Import paperwork (Israeli Ministry of Health permit): $600–$700; an exact figure to be obtained.
  • Transport to Tel Aviv through the company's logistics partners: $2,500–$4,500; not charged if the family delivers the tissue to the Tel Aviv laboratory itself.
  • Payment: an invoice (storage plus a paperwork line) is issued before the preservation protocol, so some payment is needed in advance.
  • Personalised anti-cancer vaccine: about $45,000 up to $90,000, sometimes $100,000 (quoted for Kazakhstan).
  • Oncolytic-virus therapy in Germany: about €54,000 (the virus therapy only; immunotherapy drugs extra).

What the consultant said about treatment

  • The vaccines are a paid treatment, not a clinical trial; eligibility is confirmed individually from the medical records. For this patient's GIST (KIT mutations in three exons, family statement) the consultant said they have had such patients and that the records must be reviewed.
  • Oncolytic-virus therapy is offered in a clinic in Germany; it does not need living tissue (a paraffin block could be used), can start relatively quickly from the medical reports, and is followed by immunotherapy drugs that can be obtained locally.
  • The bank does not exchange tumour tissue for research against treatment costs; it keeps tissue per patient for that patient's own vaccine.
INTERPRETATION These are statements of a company about its own service. This site has no independent evidence of benefit of such vaccines or oncolytic-virus therapy in GIST; nothing here is a recommendation. Trial and tissue questions remain open in the pending list.
